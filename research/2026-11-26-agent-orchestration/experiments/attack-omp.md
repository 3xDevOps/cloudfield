# Adversarial review of PR #1130

Reviewer: `openai-codex/gpt-6.2-astra` (omp). Fast mode: not reported by this harness; unknown. Reviewed `e3ce2123b`. Research file references below are relative to `research/2026-11-26-agent-orchestration/`; `1a`–`3c` denote the correspondingly prefixed files in `evidence/`.

## Findings, most severe first

1. **Blocker — the public draft discloses the account it claims to withhold.** (Detail shortened by the lead so this file does not repeat it.) PROPOSALS named the reviewer account while saying it was left out on purpose; evidence file 3c connected people, a named host, outages and an account's sign-in history. **Correction:** keep identifiable evidence and the account recipe private; publish arm labels and de-identified aggregates.

2. **Blocker — the cited author corrected the central cross-provider number.** “A second provider raised defect coverage from 33.6% to 57.1%” (`REPORT.md:49`). `1c:42` faithfully quotes [version 1](https://zenodo.org/records/20519585), but [version 2](https://zenodo.org/records/21328807) explicitly corrects the coverage curve: 33 artifacts, 294 confirmed issues; within an artifact, among reviewers that actually reviewed it, about 47% with one reviewer and 72% with two. The author's conclusion is **a second independent reviewer**, not evidence that switching providers beats a rerun. **Correction:** replace the figures and attribute the revised inference. Choosing a different family can be a hedge, not a measured requirement.

3. **Blocker — the proposed docs exemption discards the clearest local counterexample.** “Skip the second model for documentation-only and mechanical changes … a second reviewer on a trivial change mostly adds noise” (`REPORT.md:55`; `PROPOSALS.md:18,30`). In `3a:55–61`, #1123 is a tiny listings change (+11/−11): omp alone flagged a false claim about merge permissions, and it was fixed. #1122 is a plan; #1121 is legal copy. The two negative examples are a module-path rewrite and a CI-runner change, not a sample of harmless docs; #1127 had nits, not false alarms. `1c:45` reports **F1**, not incremental precision on trivial docs. [The paper, §§3.4, 4.4, 4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15689v1), studied synthetic mutations and bug-fix PRs with other models. **Correction:** exempt demonstrably non-semantic changes as a cost decision; keep permission, legal, installer and migration claims in scope regardless of file type. No local noise rate was measured.

4. **Blocker — an aborted run becomes a public staffing rule and a product priority.** “A session has one CPU … start subagents directly, because a workflow runs two agents at a time on one CPU” (`PROPOSALS.md:45–48`; see first-ranked change at 92–96). `experiments/README.md:52–60` records two concurrent workflow workers, then cancellation after about two minutes; there is no workflow completion time to compare. Even the pressure attribution is off: at the 06:58:45 reading (line 37), 6 min 37 s after the direct launch, six of the eight collectors had already returned. It is a rolling pressure reading, not a ten-worker steady-state measurement. `internal/scheduler/capacity.go:68–92` defaults to `min(8, NumCPU)` with overrides; `docs/environments.md:287–303` already documents per-launch CPU selection. `1b:25,29` gives configurable ceilings, not a universal two-worker rule. **Correction:** report the observed width and the cancelled comparison; measure completed matched briefs, quality and latency at several widths before ranking an override as the biggest time saver. Prefer checking this session's actual limits to raising concurrency under contention.

5. **Blocker — the eight-peer cap really existed.** “A stale ‘8 peers’ cap that the code never had” (`REPORT.md:115`, inner quotes normalized). `2b:19` quotes #1108 in the **present** tense. At `25562c81d`, `internal/coord/mailbox.go:45–48,1061` defines `maxPeers = 8` and passes it to the store; `internal/coord/mailbox_test.go:459–468` checks that peer nine gets a conflict. Commit `618a438fb` (#664) removed the cap on October 30. **Correction:** “The note described an earlier version; current sends have no distinct-peer cap.” Do not confuse it with the current default of eight *busy children* (`2a:41–44`; `internal/store/children.go:90–95`).

6. **Blocker — the benchmark averages mask configurations with the opposite result.** “Every multi-agent setup scored below a single agent on SWE-bench Verified” (`REPORT.md:11`). The range in `1c:11` is correct for **architecture means**. In the [paper, Appendix F, Table 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296), GPT-5-nano scores 5% solo vs 35% centralized; Gemini 2.0 Flash scores 15% solo vs 40% independent (20 instances). The benchmark compared ways to solve one issue, not the time to ship independent tickets. The report also omits `1c:52–55`: a manager with isolated workers beat single agents by 14.7 percentage points on Commit0 and 25.6 on PaperBench ([CAID abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21489)). These are benchmarks too, not evidence about Cloudfield production tickets. The local urgent-fix example actually used two reviewer sub-sessions; the old rule said “little,” not *no*, fan-out (`3c:17`). **Correction:** report means, variation and the opposing result; distinguish solo implementation from solo review, and “no local controlled comparison” from “no published study.”

7. **Blocker — the two known defects do not have the stated provenance.** “Two merged pull requests that each introduced a race found three weeks later” (`REPORT.md:28`). In the **parent** of #547, `175f1ad7c^:internal/cost/service.go:428–450` already reads status before locking and publishes that possibly stale state later. #547 added the `From` field, push consequence and a publication lock that did not cover the read; it did not introduce the underlying stale-read ordering. For #716, `a3171a811` merged November 5 and its fix `82edc8324` merged November 18: discovery could not have been three weeks later. `3a:70–74` describes reviews of the **fixes**, not a blind opportunity for those reviewers to discover still-unfixed bugs. **Correction:** name the historical failure each replay tests. These were selected *because* they escaped to CI; the observed one-of-two exact hits per arm is not a review escape rate. A red log plus targeted stress is a different task from an untargeted diff review.

8. **Should-fix — the reported multi-hour “delays” are elapsed times, not time attributable to stacking.** “One bad pull request fails everything queued behind it … Delays 1.4 to 5.2 h” (`REPORT.md:94`; `PROPOSALS.md:31,83–84`). `3b:53–62` measures first enqueue to merge. #1029 took 5 h 12 min **including its own later flake**; #1031 had later integration and e2e failures. `3b:68–69` counts 24 passing descendant runs alongside 50 cancelled and 35 failed. A shared base demonstrably propagated a failure; neither “everything” nor savings from serializing each lead's independent PRs follows. **Correction:** give the matched failure and total elapsed time separately. Avoid known-bad bases; present one-at-a-time as a throughput/risk tradeoff, not the measured optimum for a two-entry pipeline.

9. **Should-fix — “3 of 7” mixes unknown overlap, unknown outcomes and different review scopes.** “omp reported a blocker or factual error the Opus reviews had not in 3 of 7 pull requests” (`REPORT.md:49`). In the seven PR-body records (`3a:51–66,109–114`), #1120 and #1123 have identifiable additional findings, but #1120 compared a delta to a full diff. #1122 has five reported blockers with **unknown** overlap; #1121 is partial/pre-existing; #1124 has no omp findings recorded; two had no incremental blocker. `3c:62–68` additionally records omp work on #1125/#1126, outside that seven-row set. **Correction:** give the case classifications, label the denominator “PR bodies naming omp,” and reconcile the memory-only cases separately. Neither absence of a finding in a body nor a higher blocker count establishes a miss by another reviewer.

10. **Should-fix — the arms compare review packages, not model family at a fixed budget.** “A second run of the same model also adds findings, but fewer” (`REPORT.md:44`; used in `PROPOSALS.md:18`). The report notes longer omp runtime, but not that effort was explicitly high only for the in-session Opus arm, that tests differed, and that both omp lists hit the ten-finding reporting ceiling (`experiments/README.md:18–22,35–40`; `brief-subagent.md:17–19`; `opus-subagent-716.md:7–8`). The samples are large: `git diff --shortstat` gives 687 and 2,707 changed lines vs a local merged median of 153 (`3a:12`). Even “unique” depends on credit: on #547 the Opus report explicitly mentions overflow/replay from zero (`opus-subagent-547.md:65`); the judge counted the developed double-count consequence only for omp (`judge-547.md:22,30,45`). Crediting the shared mechanism changes omp-only from six to five and overlap from four to five. **Correction:** distinguish a first mention from a demonstrated consequence. Before preferring one cross-family run to more same-family runs, compare independent reruns in matched environments at equal cost or time, with the same tool/test allowance and a common triage rubric.

11. **Should-fix — 32 is not the number of independently validated bugs.** “The judges rejected none of the 32 findings they checked” (`REPORT.md:47`). This is true counting reviewer *submissions*: #547 has 7+9+5 = 21 checked labels but **13 of 14** distinct clusters checked (`judge-547.md:34–59`); #716 has 5+3+3 = 11 checked labels but **8 of 18** clusters checked (`judge-716.md:34–45,49–55`). Thus 21/32 distinct clusters checked, not independently replicated, with deliberate selection of blockers/top-ranked items. Of the 14 omp-only clusters in the two tables, eight were checked. One accepted #547 issue has “no failure today”; another's trigger was not traced. **Correction:** publish both denominators and the selection rule. “Judge accepted mechanism” is not “independently reproduced regression”; leave unexamined findings unknown rather than folding them into evidence of precision.

12. **Should-fix — the recommended merge wait misses a second, ordinary failure path.** “Use `--until pr:...:merged`; none needed beyond that” (`REPORT.md:90`). Besides a failed queue read, `internal/watch/wait_pr.go:87–101` explicitly ignores removal at or before the wait's creation. A lead registering after the PR has already dropped can sleep on a perfectly readable open PR. `2b:49–55,58–60` also separates the one-minute settle period from polling, seven-day expiry and protected sessions that do not wake. **Correction:** register promptly after enqueue and verify queue membership/removal time at registration; handle an already-dropped PR immediately. On an unreadable queue use an independent check, not indefinite reliance on this wait.

13. **Should-fix — commit, publish and recover are three different operations.** “Commit and push the result file, because the parent fetches only committed work” (`REPORT.md:68`); “A sandbox that dies loses what was not pushed” (`PROPOSALS.md:53`). `2b:69–74` and `internal/children/agent.go:341–353` show that fetch itself publishes a live child's committed HEAD. `docs/failure-handling.md:1004–1020` documents a recovery boot that commits and publishes even previously *uncommitted* work, with retries; data is lost if the checkout is gone before capture. **Correction:** commit for parent fetch; push for a separate remote copy or an explicit handoff requirement. Explain the actual loss condition, not a guarantee of loss on process or VM stop.

14. **Should-fix — the evidence for running every integration test on string changes is one relevant failure, not 73.** “Run the integration suite locally … 73 of 73 origin queue failures in queue-only jobs had a green pull request run” (`REPORT.md:53`; `PROPOSALS.md:62–64,100`). `3b:40–49` includes e2e, Windows, KVM and release jobs in that count. No cross-tab of string-changing PRs vs integration failures was collected. #1025 does demonstrate a specific consumer assertion a PR check missed. **Correction:** run the integration tests that consume the changed output; for that case `make test-integration INTEGRATION_PKGS=./internal/server INTEGRATION_RUN='^TestIntegrationTerminalExec$'` (`Makefile:235–244`). Reserve the full suite for broad changes or unknown consumers; do not estimate its benefit from 73 failures in unrelated jobs.

15. **Should-fix — “refused” does not mean “temporarily out of capacity.”** “Retry a queued sub-session that admission refused, with backoff, before telling the parent” (`PROPOSALS.md:103`). `2a:45,58–60` distinguishes policy, permissions, budget and pressure. `internal/children/dispatch.go:75–103,114–125,148–151` uses the refusal path for changed ownership, a disabled account, failed authorization, budget and launch errors. Waiting before telling the parent of a permanent denial wastes time; retrying all pressure failures can add load to an already distressed host (`3c:44`). **Correction:** report permanent refusals immediately. If adding retries, classify transient capacity errors, bound attempts/time, recheck authorization and cancellation, and expose that the child is waiting rather than silently delaying the parent.

## Claims checked and found sound

- The two Opus arms share three clusters on #716 (6, 14, 16). Their union is 7+6−3 = 10; omp intersects it on 6 and 8. Under the judge's stated clustering rule the #547 unions are eight Opus vs ten omp, four shared (`experiments/judge-{547,716}.md`, cluster tables; label key in `experiments/README.md:46`).
- About 4.7× the sub-session cost and 3× the aggregate launch-to-report time: 11.752 / 2.481 = 4.737 for metered cost (the first total rounds to 11.75, not 11.76); (690+720) / (195+278) = 2.98 for seconds (`experiments/README.md:28–33`). Neither is a multiplier against in-session Opus.
- The queue's pass/fail-only failure share is 149/(316+149) = 32.0%; 1,404 s vs 1,421 s are 23.4 vs 23.7 minutes for failed and successful median durations (`3b:19–25`).
- The durable outcome summary is capped at 4 KiB, not cut to 300 characters; the parent inbox message contains the stored summary (`2b:29–34`; `internal/protocol/coord.go:45–46`; `internal/coord/mailbox.go:824–837`).
- Both proposed team-memory bodies fit the byte limit: 917 and 971 UTF-8 bytes without fence/newline, against 1,024 (`PROPOSALS.md:11–19`; `internal/protocol/memory.go:40`).
